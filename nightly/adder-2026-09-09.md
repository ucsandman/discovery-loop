# Binary Adder-Tree PB Encoding — 2026-09-09

## What was built
`~/workspace/discovery-loop/nightly/satenc2_adder.py` — replaces the
`PBEnc.seqcounter` sum constraints in `satenc2.py` with a ripple-carry
binary adder tree.

For each tensor equation, sums the 2R literals (`p_pos[r]`, `~p_neg[r]`)
using a chain of half-adders into a binary counter, then fixes the
output bits to `t+R` with unit clauses. Each half-adder is 2 vars / 7 clauses
(Tseitin for XOR + AND). Bit-width grows as `bit_length(count)` as literals
are added, minimizing variables.

## CNF size comparison

| Case | Seqcounter (clauses / vars) | Adder (clauses / vars) | Reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| n=2, R=7, K=2 | 24,480 / 7,944 | 33,017 / 9,681 | -35% / -22% (worse) |
| n=3, R=26, K=2 | 2,091,510 / 673,068 | 1,822,780 / 526,621 | +13% / +22% (better) |

The adder wins on vars at n=3 scale (22% fewer) but loses at n=2 scale
where seqcounter's O(n·k) is already small and the adder's per-bit
overhead dominates. Build time is comparable (3.9s vs ~5s for n=3).

## Correctness (n=2)
- Adder encoding: SAT in 1.5s (vs 0.9s seqcounter), `verify.py` confirms feasible.
- The encoding is sound. The slowdown at n=2 is due to larger CNF, not a bug.

## n=3 results (target: R=26)
| Run | CNF | Result |
|---|---|---|
| R=26, K=2, adder | 1,822,780 clauses / 526,621 vars | **TIMEOUT** at 660s (no SAT/UNSAT) |

## Conclusion
The adder-tree encoding **did not** make n=3 R=26 tractable. It reduces
variables by 22% and clauses by 13% vs seqcounter, but Cadical still cannot
finish within 11 minutes. The problem is not just encoding size — the
combinatorial search space remains too large for the solver.

## Not tried
- Adder + symmetry breaking combined: both individually timed out; the
  combination was deemed unlikely to succeed within the time-box.
- The 22% var reduction suggests the adder is a better base encoding for
  future work (LNS subproblems, CEGAR), but it is not sufficient alone.
