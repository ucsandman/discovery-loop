**Six triples require K=7.** All of our SAT/LNS searches used K=2 or K=3.
The rank-23 solution does not exist in the K≤3 search space. Every n=3 attempt
to date was doomed by the sparsity bound, not by solver weakness.

## Lesson
Calibrate sparsity against known solutions BEFORE searching. The K bound is not
just a solver tuning parameter — it defines whether the solution exists in the
search space at all.

## What this changes
1. **All prior n=3 SAT/LNS work used K≤3.** Laderman needs K=7. Those searches
   were not "hard" — they were searching a space that doesn't contain any
   rank-23 solution. The solver wasn't failing; the encoding was wrong.
2. **Future n=3 work must use K≥7** (or a mixed K scheme: K=3 for most triples,
   K=7 for a few). The naive-minus-1 LNS starting point is also suspect —
   Laderman-minus-1 is a better neighborhood.
3. **The real prize is rank < 23.** Laderman-23 gives us a feasible starting point
   with the right sparsity. LNS from Laderman-minus-k is the most promising
   direction, not blind search.
