# Symmetry Breaking for n=3 SAT Encoding — 2026-09-09

## What was built
`~/workspace/discovery-loop/nightly/satenc2_sym.py` — extends `satenc2.py` (imported, not modified) with two symmetry-breaking constraint families:

1. **Permutation breaking (26! symmetries):** T_0 ≤ T_1 ≤ ... ≤ T_{R-1} lexicographic over
   each triple flattened to its 3n² ternary entries in {-1,0,1}.
   - Prefix-equality auxiliaries eq[r][j] with full Tseitin reification
     (eq[j+1] ↔ eq[j] ∧ (neg equal) ∧ (pos equal)).
   - Ternary ≤ enforced by 2 clauses per entry:
     `[-eq, neg_x, -neg_y]` (forbids x≥0, y=-1) and
     `[-eq, neg_x, -pos_x, pos_y]` (forbids x=1, y=0).
   - 14 clauses per entry per adjacent pair — 9,450 clauses for n=3, R=26.
2. **Sign breaking (4^26 symmetries):** each triple's UVW product is invariant under
   (U,V,W)→(-U,-V,W), (U,-V,-W), (-U,V,-W).
   - Rule S1: first nonzero entry of W is positive.
   - Rule S2: first nonzero entry of flattened (U,V) is positive.
   - Via "prefix-all-zero" (fz) chains; ~3.5k clauses for n=3, R=26.

Total symmetry overhead for n=3, R=26: **13,037 clauses** on top of 2,091,510 base.

## Verification (n=2, R=7, K=2)
- SAT in **1.2s** (vs 0.9s without sym breaking — tiny overhead on trivial case).
- `verify.py`: **feasible: True**.
- Returned triples confirmed lexicographically sorted, W first-nonzero positive,
  (U,V) first-nonzero positive → constraints are active, not vacuous, and sound.

## Conclusion
Symmetry breaking **did not** make R=26 tractable. The 26! permutation and 4^26 sign
symmetries are real, and the breaking constraints are correct and cheap (13k clauses
vs 2.09M), but the solver still cannot finish the 2M-clause pseudo-boolean sum
encoding within 10+ minutes. The bottleneck is the PB sum encoding (729 equations ×
52 literals via seqcounter), not the symmetries.

## Next leads (not tried)
- Tighter PB encoding: binary adder tree instead of seqcounter for the
  Σ(p_pos + ¬p_neg) = t+R constraints — could cut ~1M variables.
- Structure-aware LNS on top of the sym-broken encoding (smaller subproblems
  may benefit more from the symmetry pruning than the full R=26).
- CEGAR: solve a relaxed problem, add violated tensor equations lazily.
