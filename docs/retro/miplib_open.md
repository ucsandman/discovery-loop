## 2026-09-05 06:01 miplib_open: iters 0-6, spend $7.67, champion -0.3354, beating best-known: none

### What worked
- Nothing became champion. Seven iterations, seven rejections, $7.67. The only thing that "worked" is the seed's own ordering: HiGHS-default incumbent then random-fix LNS, which already holds the n37xx family at 4.5–6.2% (vs 8–9.5% for plain HiGHS at 120 s) and ties assign1-10-4.

### What didn't
- iter 1 (adaptive neighbourhood portfolio, −0.4508): −0.115 vs seed. Portfolio bookkeeping plus an LP-relaxation solve for RINS on the 25k-row neos-1423785 ate sub-MIP time; on the n37xx family RINS is useless because the LP relaxation sets every binary fractional at x/M, so it agrees with nothing.
- iter 2 (objective cutoff on every sub-MIP, −1.3893): −1.05, i.e. one target scored as an outright failure plus drift. With the original objective kept, a cutoff sub-MIP can only end by *proving* infeasibility, which never happens before the time limit on 1000 free binaries; most likely the cutoff sign was also wrong on the maximisation path or the solver had no solution to write when HiGHS returned kInfeasible. Never let a change touch the save path and the search in the same iteration.
- iter 3 (constraint-graph block neighbourhoods, −0.3845) and iter 4 (support-biased neighbourhood, −0.3932): duplicates of each other in effect — both pick *which* integers to free. On n37xx the column–row graph is 5000 private variable-bound rows hanging off 150 dense coupling rows, so BFS blocks are either one arc or the whole instance, and "active" binaries (open arcs, ~150–200 of 5000) are exactly the ones the incumbent already agrees on. Both within ~0.05 of seed: noise-level, no signal.
- iter 5 (callback stop-on-first-improvement, −0.6483): −0.31, too large for LNS drift across 10 targets; at least one target regressed hard. Stopping on the first tiny improvement means every sub-MIP pays full presolve+root for a marginal gain, and the callback almost certainly also fired during phase 1, cutting off the warm start.
- iter 6 (many short time-targeted sub-MIPs, multiplicative k adaptation, −0.3491): −0.014 vs seed, pure noise. Duplicate of the seed's own additive adaptation with a different step schedule.

### Lessons
- All six rejects were the same kind: "choose which integers to fix and for how long" inside HiGHS sub-MIP LNS. That axis is exhausted; the n37xx gap never moved below 4.5% under any of them.
- Five n37xx instances (5000 binary/continuous var-bound pairs + 150 coupling rows: fixed-charge transportation) carry 0.26 of the 0.29 total gap. An idea not measured on n37 first is spending $1/iteration on the 10% that doesn't matter.
- The champion's single-run total (−0.3354) is worse than the scoreboard's per-target sum (−0.29): seed-to-seed noise is ≈0.04, so iters 3/4/6 were decided by noise. A change must win by ≥0.1 (≥2% on each n37) before the gate means anything.
- One failed target costs 1.0 = 3× the entire remaining gap (iters 2, 5). Any change to the save/verify path or to phase 1 must be wrapped so the phase-1 incumbent always survives on disk.
- On n37xx a sub-MIP with ≥1000 free binaries times out (fix drifts to 0.95, neighbourhoods shrink to 250 binaries); HiGHS B&B is the wrong oracle for this structure, the 150-row LP is the cheap one.
- Time goes: 150 s phase-1 B&B that plateaus around 120 s, then ~15 sub-MIPs of 45 s each. Nobody has yet looked at the `trace` field the champion writes to see whether the LNS curve is flat by 300 s.
